The vanilla bean

A little bean with a remarkable aroma.

Vanilla is the cured fruit of an orchid in the genus Vanilla. Its tiny seeds and aromatic compounds make the whole cured pod valuable for baking, desserts, beverages, chocolate, ice cream, extracts and premium culinary applications.

Our catalogue focuses on two distinctive profiles: Planifolia for a rich and classic vanilla character, and Tahitian Vanilla for a softer, floral and fruity aroma.

Tahitian Vanilla

Vanilla × tahitensis / Tahitensis

A specialty vanilla appreciated for its delicate floral character, fruity notes and distinctive aromatic profile. It is especially attractive in pastry, creams, fruit-forward desserts and fragrance applications.

From orchid to bean

Why curing matters

Vanilla develops its familiar aroma through careful harvesting and curing rather than simply drying a fresh pod.

01

Pollination

Vanilla flowers have a short flowering window and are commonly pollinated by hand in cultivation.

02

Maturation

Green pods remain on the vine for months while the seeds and tissues develop.

03

Curing

Heat, sweating, slow drying and conditioning transform the green pod into aromatic cured vanilla.

04

Selection

Beans can then be sorted by length, appearance, moisture, flexibility and intended use.
